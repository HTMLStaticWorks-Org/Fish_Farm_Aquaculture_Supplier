PRACTICAL FIELD METHODS

Best practices for profitable, resilient harvests.

Developed by our agronomists and water biologists based on commercial operations across freshwater, brackish, and marine farming clusters.

Phase 01

Pond Preparation & Pre-Stocking Conditioning

A successful crop cycle begins well before fingerlings arrive. De-silting, soil drying, and proper liming establish a healthy aquatic foundation.

  • Bottom Soil Drying: Drain and sun-dry pond bottom until hairline cracks appear (10–14 days) to oxidize noxious anaerobic gases.
  • Liming Calculations: Apply agricultural limestone (CaCO₃) or quicklime (CaO) according to soil pH to stabilize alkalinity (>120 mg/L).
  • Screened Water Filling: Pass incoming water through 60-mesh filtration screens to block wild predatory fish, eggs, and unwanted pathogens.
Phase 02

Dissolved Oxygen & Daily Water Chemistry

Water quality directly determines growth rates and disease resistance. Regular parameter tracking prevents sudden mortality spikes.

  • Dissolved Oxygen (DO): Maintain DO above 5.0 mg/L. Schedule paddlewheel aeration during critical early-morning hours (2:00 AM – 6:00 AM).
  • pH & Ammonia Control: Keep pH between 7.5 and 8.3 with diurnal fluctuations under 0.5 units to prevent un-ionized ammonia (NH₃) toxicity.
  • Water Transparency: Use a Secchi disk to maintain plankton bloom visibility between 30 cm and 40 cm for optimal biological balance.
Phase 03

Precision Feeding & Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)

Feed accounts for 60–70% of operational expenditure. Precision rationing protects water clarity while accelerating biomass gain.

  • Check-Tray Audits: Inspect feed check-trays 90 minutes post-feeding. If more than 5% remains uneaten, reduce next ration by 10%.
  • Temperature Adjustments: During excessive midday heat (>33°C) or cold spells (<22°C), scale back rations as fish metabolic rates shift.
  • Automated Dispersal: Utilize solar automatic feeders to distribute small portions across 6–8 intervals for peak gut absorption.
Phase 04

Biosecure Harvest & Cold-Chain Grading

Preserve market quality and command premium pricing with stress-free harvesting and instantaneous temperature-controlled chilling.

  • Pre-Harvest Fasting: Withhold feed 24 hours prior to harvest to completely clear the digestive tract, ensuring odor-free, firm flesh.
  • Knotless Harvesting Nets: Use knotless drag nets to avoid skin abrasions, scale loss, and slime layer damage during netting.
  • Slurry Ice Immersion: Plunge live harvested fish immediately into ice slurry (0°C to 2°C) to lock in peak farm freshness and shelf life.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Aquaculture Farming Questions & Answers.

Quick answers to common questions about pond aeration, stocking densities, and water chemistry.

How much paddlewheel aeration horsepower is needed per acre? +

For standard semi-intensive ponds (stocking density 10,000–15,000 fish/acre), 1.5 to 2.0 HP of paddlewheel aeration per acre is recommended. For high-density intensive cultures (>25,000 fish/acre or shrimp), calculate 1 HP for every 400–500 kg of expected biomass.

What is the best time of day to run pond aerators? +

During the day, phytoplankton generate oxygen via photosynthesis. The most critical aeration window is from midnight until sunrise (2:00 AM – 6:30 AM), when both fish and plankton consume oxygen. Run secondary aeration for 1–2 hours during hot afternoons to break thermal water stratification.

How can I prevent ammonia spikes in biofloc and lined ponds? +

Maintain a Carbon-to-Nitrogen (C:N) ratio of 12:1 to 15:1 by dosing organic carbon sources such as jaggery or molasses. Ensure continuous aeration (>6 mg/L DO) so heterotrophic nitrifying bacteria can assimilate ammonium into microbial protein.

What is an ideal Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) for commercial farming? +

With high-grade extruded floating feed, modern Tilapia farms target an FCR between 1.15 and 1.30. In Pacific White Shrimp (Vannamei), target an FCR between 1.20 and 1.40. Regular check-tray audits prevent overfeeding and keep FCR optimal.
